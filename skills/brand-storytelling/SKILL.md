# Brand Storytelling

Ten lessons paraphrased from *Shoe Dog* (full file: `knowledge/brand-storytelling-shoe-dog.md`). Each lesson is Knight's story; each "AI today" line is this repo's application.

## The ten lessons

1. **Lead with a belief, not a product spec.** The venture began as a conviction that running could make the world better. → Anchor marketing in a belief about the user's life getting better; if the team can't state it in one sentence, the campaign has nothing to stand on.
2. **Sell where the believers already gather.** Knight sold from his trunk at track meets after stores turned him away. → Demo live in the rooms where practitioners already argue about their craft; one working demo in front of the right 50 people beats a generic launch post.
3. **Let the community co-create the product.** Johnson's near-daily field letters and Bowerman's teardowns were product input. → Tight feedback loop with early users; publish "you asked, we changed" notes each cycle.
4. **Innovate from ordinary observation.** The waffle sole came from a waffle iron over breakfast. → Hunt for product ideas in users' everyday workarounds, not only in competitor feature lists.
5. **Ship imperfect — and let honesty carry the launch.** The first Nikes had flawed leather and crooked logos; buyers ordered because Blue Ribbon told the truth. → Launch a beta with known limits stated plainly.
6. **Name and mark: cheap, fast, meaningful, deadline-forced.** The Swoosh cost $35; "Nike" was chosen under a factory deadline. → Don't over-invest in naming before fit; test shortness, pronounceability, and a one-line retellable meaning.
7. **Own the brand; don't rent someone else's.** Onitsuka cut Blue Ribbon off in 1972; Knight called it liberation. → Plan the owned-brand exit (domain, list, distribution) before a platform dependency becomes a crisis.
8. **Endorse effort and identity, not just fame.** Prefontaine mattered for how he ran, and because he was local. → Partner with creators who embody the effort your product serves, even if they're small.
9. **Growth can kill you — cash flow is the real scoreboard.** Demand was strong for years while survival depended on bankers. → Report cost per retained user and payback alongside vanity growth.
10. **The product is the ad.** The waffle trainer's look drew hundreds of thousands of customers; ads didn't. → Spend first on a product moment users can see and describe; ads scale a story, they can't invent one.

## Rules

- Story first, channels second. A belief, a community, and a product moment — in that order — before any media plan.
- Never borrow a story the product doesn't already tell. If the demo doesn't make someone lean in, no ad will fix it.
- Cite the book honestly: the Jordan deal and "Just Do It" are *not* in Shoe Dog (see the knowledge file). Don't attribute them to it.
- Keep the numbers honest: the $35 Swoosh is the payment for the logo work; later payments/stock to the designer are a separate claim needing a separate source.
